Multi-currency settlement
for AI agents.

Your agent holds an account on Sera, not a wallet on a chain. It pays in any of 40 stablecoins across 22 fiat currencies — USD, SGD, MYR, JPY, EUR, GBP, BRL, MXN, IDR and more — and the receiver gets its own. Settlement is instant, and every payment comes with a provable receipt.

Build a cross-border payment app, an FX trading dashboard, a prediction market on FX, a treasury rebalancer, an x402-paid API, an AI agent — anything that needs a real FX layer underneath. No chain IDs, no RPC endpoints, no gas token.

The problem

Single-currency rails
break at the border.

An agent paying a Singapore vendor in a single dollar-denominated stablecoin pushes FX risk onto the recipient every cycle. An agent settling payroll in Manila that way means someone, somewhere, is converting and absorbing the spread.

And every agent that wants to move money becomes an infrastructure engineer: chain IDs, RPC endpoints, a gas token to keep topped up, bridge hops, confirmations to wait on.

The agentic payment stack today assumes one global currency. Real settlement doesn't work that way. So Sera is its own chain. The currencies are issued in the ledger, a filled quote is final the moment it fills, mandates are enforced in the block rather than by the client — and the agent never sees a chain ID, an RPC endpoint or a gas price.

Why an appchain

Sera is its own chain.
Here's what that buys an agent.

Settlement used to be something Sera did on a general-purpose chain. Now it is the chain — which removes four problems an agent developer would otherwise inherit.

Instant finality

A filled quote is done

No confirmation count to wait out, no reorg window, no pending state for your agent to poll. The fill is final in the block that contains it.

No gas token

Fees come out of the swap

Taken in the stablecoin you are already moving. Nothing to top up, nothing to monitor, and no payment that fails because a balance in some unrelated asset ran dry.

Mandates

Enforced by the chain

An agent key cannot exceed its cap, its pair allowlist or its destination allowlist. The limit lives in the block — not in a client your agent could be talked out of.

Matching

Proven in every block

The order book is the chain, so every block carries proof that the fills followed the published rules. You never have to take the operator's word for the price you got.

Non-custodial, stated plainly
Balances are proven to Ethereum

Every state transition carries a validity proof settled on L1. Your balance is not an entry in a database we ask you to trust.

You can leave from L1

If Sera stops responding, withdraw directly from Ethereum. request_forced_withdrawal is exposed as a tool whether or not you ever need it.

Sera cannot freeze or reorder

No seizure, no censorship of your intents, no quiet reordering of your fills. The data to reconstruct balances is published, not held.

Account model

and a mandate in between.

01

Fund the account

The owner creates a Sera account and funds it — an L1 deposit, native in-ledger issuance, or a deposit address that accepts USDC through an intent bridge.

02

Authorise an agent key

The owner key attaches a mandate: a cap in the owner's home currency, the pairs it may trade, where it may send, when it expires.

03

Quote and submit

The agent calls get_quote, gets a firm quote with the KYT/KYA result attached, countersigns, submits. Instant finality.

04

Take the receipt

Sera returns a signed settlement receipt. It is the agent's proof-of-payment for AP2 and the owner's audit trail.

05

Pay out

Stay in-ledger to the receiver's Sera account, withdraw to any L1 address, or route to an off-ramp partner through the open-sourced gateway.

Agents never see a chain ID, an RPC or a gas price. That is the pitch to agent developers.
Receipts

Every payment comes with
something you can check.

A transaction hash tells you something happened. A Sera receipt tells you what was traded, at what rate, under which authority, and against a state root you can verify yourself.

get_receipt → settlement receipt
{
  "receipt_id":  "rcpt_01JQ8M4K2P7V",
  "account":     "sera1q9f0wner...",
  "agent_key":   "sera1qk3agent...",
  "pair":        "XSGD/MYRT",
  "rate":        "3.4821",
  "amount_in":   "10000.00 XSGD",
  "amount_out":  "34821.00 MYRT",
  "fee":         "8.70 XSGD",
  "screening":   { "kyt": "pass", "kya": "pass" },
  "settled_at":  "2026-11-08T04:12:55Z",
  "block":       4821993,
  "state_root":  "0x9f2c7ae1...",
  "mandate":     { "id": "mnd_01JQ8K",
                   "remaining": "40000.00 SGD" }
}
pair · rate · amounts · fee

What was actually traded and at what price — including the fee, taken in the stablecoin you swapped rather than in a gas token.

screening

The KYT/KYA verdict as of the moment of settlement, bound into the receipt rather than filed somewhere else afterwards.

block · state_root

Anchors the receipt to state proven on Ethereum. verify_receipt checks it against the L1 state root for you.

agent_key · mandate

Which key acted, under whose authority, and how much of its cap is left. An owner reading the audit trail can tell those apart.

settled_at · receipt_id

The timestamp and identifier an AP2 proof-of-payment needs. export_receipts hands the owner the full set for an audit.

55 tools live · 13 more on the appchain

Every primitive an
FX-native agent needs.

Each tool is callable from any MCP host, the bundled CLI, or programmatically. Full API reference → with method signatures, params, and response shapes. Groups marked · appchain are the account, mandate and receipt surface — v2 of the MCP; v1 installs keep working untouched.

Discovery 6 tools — list_currencies · get_markets · doctor · search_coins · get_coin_metadata · get_coin_history
Live token registry from Sera's /tokens (40 stablecoins) and the active-market catalog. Search and query single-coin metadata via search_coins and get_coin_metadata, or pull coin history via get_coin_history. Pair existence ≠ tradeable now — use scan_markets to find what's actually quotable. doctor is a one-call self-check — API health, network sanity, signer mode, policy summary.
Pricing & analytics 4 tools — get_fx_rate · compare_to_external_fx · multi_source_mid · spread_radar
Reference FX rates with bias detection. compare_to_external_fx diffs Sera against Frankfurter (ECB). multi_source_mid takes the median across three free providers. spread_radar flags pair asymmetry and triangular drift across a basket — runs without any liquidity.
Liquidity probing 9 tools — scan_markets · find_deals · maker_quote_ladder · probe_depth · round_trip_cost · infer_book · market_health · fx_quote_diff · compare_corridors
find_deals is the headline: scans markets in parallel, diffs each against external mid, ranks the deals. probe_depth characterizes price impact at a size ladder; round_trip_cost reveals the maker hedge floor; infer_book reconstructs a synthetic order book. market_health checks corridor quotability, fx_quote_diff shows the reference-vs-executable gap, compare_corridors ranks source corridors for a target output, and maker_quote_ladder projects maker earnings across a 5→200 bps ladder.
Quote & plan 6 tools — get_quote · prepare_swap · quote_recipient_amount · find_cheapest_settlement_path · batch_quote · limit_watcher
Single-use quote + EIP-712 Intent (get_quote / prepare_swap) — pass simulate:true to probe with no wallet. quote_recipient_amount inverts it — the input required for an exact target output. find_cheapest_settlement_path picks the cheapest source; batch_quote fetches up to 50 in one round-trip; limit_watcher polls until a target rate hits.
Execution 2 tools — execute_swap · convert_and_send
execute_swap submits the signed swap (destructive) with uuid binding + server-derived USD notional caps. convert_and_send quotes, executes, and delivers in one call — local-signer mode only. Both are gated behind SERA_ENABLE_EXECUTION_TOOLS and enforce the policy caps.
Maker & orders 9 tools — place_order · cancel_order · cancel_all_orders · place_vl_batch · cancel_vl_batch · get_order · list_orders · get_fills · get_fills_for_order
Full CLOB order lifecycle. place_order submits an agent-signed limit order; place_vl_batch posts a capital-efficient multi-pair Virtual Liquidity batch. cancel_order / cancel_all_orders / cancel_vl_batch retire them. get_order, list_orders, get_fills, get_fills_for_order read status and fills. Requires SERA_API_KEY.
Treasury 6 tools — get_balances · treasury_value · exposure_report · rebalance_plan · pay_invoice · settlement_status
Multi-wallet aggregation (get_balances, treasury_value), currency exposure_report, target-weight rebalance_plan, and pay_invoice — "I owe X in currency Y, what's the cheapest source asset?" settlement_status tracks a trade through Sera's /orders. Requires SERA_API_KEY.
Account & keys · appchain 6 tools — create_account · create_agent_key · set_mandate · revoke_key · list_keys · mandate_remaining
The two-tier key model. create_account opens a Sera account from the owner key; create_agent_key mints a key for one agent and set_mandate attaches its limits — notional cap in the owner's home currency, pair allowlist, destination allowlist, expiry. mandate_remaining is what an agent should call before planning a large settlement; list_keys and revoke_key are the owner's controls. The chain enforces the mandate, so the MCP sets and mirrors it rather than being the thing that holds the line.
Funding · appchain 3 tools — deposit_address · deposit_status · native_issuance_status
deposit_address returns the account's L1 deposit address, plus the bridged-in address that accepts USDC from Base, Arbitrum and other EVM chains through an intent bridge landing on the L1 deposit contract. deposit_status tracks a deposit through to credit. native_issuance_status covers the stablecoins minted directly on the appchain — MYRT, XSGD and the other issuers you already work with — where there is no bridge leg at all.
Receipts · appchain 3 tools — get_receipt · verify_receipt · export_receipts
get_receipt fetches the signed settlement receipt for a trade — pair, rate, amounts, screening result, timestamp, block and state root. verify_receipt checks it against the L1 state root, so a counterparty can confirm a payment without trusting Sera. export_receipts hands the owner the full set for an audit trail or an AP2 proof-of-payment bundle.
Exit · appchain 1 tool — request_forced_withdrawal
The escape hatch. If the sequencer stops responding, request_forced_withdrawal starts a withdrawal from Ethereum itself, with a bounded delay. It is exposed whether or not anyone ever calls it — this is the non-custodial claim made callable.
Account & funds 5 tools — build_approve · build_deposit · build_transfer · send_tx · send_transfer
Build the unsigned transactions for the L1 funding leg — ERC-20 build_approve, vault build_deposit (optionally with an EIP-2612 permit), and build_transfer — then broadcast the signed result with send_tx / send_transfer. Token approvals and permits live here and only here: once funds are inside Sera, payment is an intent against a ledger balance with no approval and no gas. The agent signs; nothing is custodial.
Withdraw 3 tools — withdraw_request · withdraw_build · withdraw_send
The staged non-custodial withdrawal flow — withdraw_request gets an executor co-signature, withdraw_build constructs the unsigned tx, and withdraw_send broadcasts the signed one. Funds leave the Vault only under a signature you produce.
History 3 tools — fx_history · fx_volatility · corridor_pnl
OHLC isn't published as a primitive. Set SERA_HISTORY_DB to a SQLite path and the MCP logs every fx_rate + quote call it serves. Over time, your install becomes its own price feed — series, volatility, mark-to-market PnL queryable.
Signing & debug 2 tools — verify_signature · permit_metadata
verify_signature tests an EIP-712 signature without placing an order; permit_metadata checks EIP-2612 permit support for a token before you build a gasless deposit. Dry-run helpers for wiring up signing.

x402 on Sera — Path D

Sera is the facilitator now.

x402 used to mean running your own service against someone else's chain. With settlement on the appchain, Sera is the facilitator itself — so a managed endpoint arrives on agents.sera.cx at appchain launch, and self-hosting stays for operators who want to hold their own key.

Receiving side
from any chain

Take a standard x402 payment, keep your own currency

An agent anywhere pays USDC over plain x402 — on Base, on Ethereum mainnet, with no Sera knowledge and no Sera account. The payment lands in Sera's gateway, converts in-ledger, and credits the merchant in XSGD, MYRT, EURC or any of the 40.

USDC in converted in-ledger merchant currency out
Native side
account → account

Sera accounts pay 402 invoices to each other

Both sides already hold Sera accounts, so a 402 invoice settles in-ledger: instant, no bridge, no approvals, no gas token. The fee comes out of the stablecoin being paid, and the payer's agent key stays inside its mandate.

instant no bridge mandate-bounded receipt

Endpoints

Managed — agents.sera.cx · at appchain launch
  • POST /x402/invoice — issue a 402 invoice
  • POST /x402/swap — 402 → pay → 200
  • POST /x402/quote — quote-only, no payment
Self-hosted — your infrastructure
  • GET / — service-discovery JSON
  • GET /health — ops probe

Run modes

  • testnet — developer mode
    Points at the Sera appchain testnet and its faucet for test stablecoins. Nothing is mocked: the payment verification and the swap leg are the same code path that runs in production, against a chain where the money isn't real.
  • live — operator-gated
    For self-hosting only. Requires your own deployment, appchain testnet end-to-end verification, and explicit operator approval before handling real settlement. Review the service README and security model first. The managed endpoint needs none of this.
